# Audio and subtitle offsets

From the feature-parity list (`docs/plans/feature-parity.md`): "Audio and subtitle delay", marked
Missing. Kodi has audio and subtitle offsets of its own, but they live in Kodi's video OSD, which
can't be reached while Arc's player window owns the remote. A TV with a soundbar often needs the
sound moved a little to match the lips, and a subtitle file can be early or late.

## What changed

- The player's **Playback menu** gains **Audio Offset**, and **Subtitle Offset** for a title with
  subtitles, each showing the offset now ("None", "50 ms later", "1.5 s earlier"). Each opens a
  list in Kodi's steps, the one in use ticked:
  - audio: 25 ms steps up to 100 ms, then up to 500 ms either way;
  - subtitles: 100 ms steps up to 200 ms, then up to 5 s either way.
- Kodi has no call to set an offset, so choosing one sends Kodi's own AudioDelay/SubtitleDelay
  Plus/Minus actions, one step at a time, from Kodi's value now (`Player.AudioDelay`). Kodi shows
  its small slider for a moment as it moves, which also confirms the change.
- **The audio offset belongs to the device** (a TV and its soundbar), so it's kept in the add-on's
  profile folder (with the session, `audio_offset_ms`) and put back whenever a stream starts. A
  **subtitle offset belongs to the title**: it's kept while that title plays (a conversion restart
  puts it back) and dropped for the next.
- The maths (Kodi's labels to milliseconds, steps, the lists, the words) is in `arc/playback.py`,
  tested in `tests/test_playback.py`.

This builds on the Playback menu's submenus from the player-extras PR (#6) and the rest of the
open stack; the branch starts from #10 (`claude/kodi-together`).

## Checked

- `python3 -m pytest -q`: 96 passed. `python3 tools/check.py`: all good.
- Kodi 20 on a virtual display against a scratch Arc (not real hardware, and no audio device, so
  lip-sync itself wasn't heard):
  - first, that Kodi's delay actions still reach the player with Arc's window over the video;
  - Playback → Audio Offset → "50 ms later": Kodi's `Player.AudioDelay` became 0.050 s and it was
    saved; playing again (and after restarting Kodi) the stream started at 0.050 s ("audio offset
    applied");
  - with a subtitle file beside the test video, Playback → Subtitle Offset → "500 ms later":
    `Player.SubtitleDelay` became 0.500 s. Without subtitles the row doesn't show.

## Logging

- "audio offset changed" / "subtitles offset changed" at info; the value and "audio offset
  applied: N ms" at debug.

## Limits

- Kodi's own "Set as default for all media" for offsets isn't offered; the add-on keeps the audio
  offset itself.
- Not checked on real hardware or with sound.
